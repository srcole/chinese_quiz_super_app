# Identity with 是 | Subject + 是 + noun | review
Use 是 to identify a person or thing with a noun, such as a job or relationship. Chinese does not need an article equivalent to 'a' before a job title.
我是学生。|I am a student.
她是老师。|She is a teacher.
他是医生。|He is a doctor.
这是我的书。|This is my book.
那是你的杯子。|That is your cup.
我是她的朋友。|I am her friend.
他是我爸爸。|He is my dad.||他是我的爸爸。
她是我妈妈。|She is my mom.||她是我的妈妈。
今天是星期一。|Today is Monday.
明天是我的生日。|Tomorrow is my birthday.

# Denying identity with 不是 | Subject + 不是 + noun | review
Put 不 directly before 是 to say that someone or something is not a particular person or thing. Do not use 没 to negate identity.
我不是老师。|I am not a teacher.
她不是医生。|She is not a doctor.
他不是我的哥哥。|He is not my older brother.||他不是我哥哥。
这不是我的手机。|This is not my phone.
那不是你的书。|That is not your book.
今天不是星期天。|Today is not Sunday.||今天不是星期日。
明天不是我的生日。|Tomorrow is not my birthday.
这里不是学校。|This place is not a school.
这不是茶。|This is not tea.
他们不是我的同学。|They are not my classmates.

# Having things with 有 | Person + 有 + noun | review
Use 有 to say that a person has something. Put a number and measure word before a counted object; 有 itself does not change for different subjects.
我有一本书。|I have a book.
她有一只小狗。|She has a puppy.
他有两个姐姐。|He has two older sisters.
我们有三张票。|We have three tickets.
你有一个好朋友。|You have a good friend.
我有一辆自行车。|I have a bicycle.
弟弟有一个苹果。|My younger brother has an apple.||我弟弟有一个苹果。
老师有一支笔。|The teacher has a pen.
我有时间。|I have time.
我们有一个问题。|We have a question.

# Not having things with 没有 | Person + 没有 + noun | review
Negate possession with 没有, not 不有. A bare noun after 没有 usually means that the person has none of that thing.
我没有车。|I do not have a car.
她没有弟弟。|She does not have a younger brother.
他没有手机。|He does not have a phone.
我们没有钱。|We do not have money.
我没有哥哥。|I do not have an older brother.
妹妹没有电脑。|My younger sister does not have a computer.||我妹妹没有电脑。
他们没有孩子。|They do not have children.
我今天没有课。|I do not have classes today.||今天我没有课。
我们没有牛奶。|We do not have milk.
她没有自行车。|She does not have a bicycle.

# Adding a matching fact with 也 | Subject + 也 + predicate | review
Place 也 before the verb or adjective phrase to mean 'also' or 'too'. It follows the subject, rather than appearing at the end as English 'too' often does.
我也是学生。|I am a student too.
她也喜欢猫。|She also likes cats.
他也喝茶。|He drinks tea too.
我也有妹妹。|I also have a younger sister.
妈妈也在家。|Mom is at home too.
我们也去学校。|We are going to school too.
这本书也很好。|This book is good too.
弟弟也很高。|My younger brother is tall too.||我弟弟也很高。
我也不吃肉。|I do not eat meat either.
她也没有车。|She does not have a car either.

# Joining nouns with 和 | Noun + 和 + noun | review
Use 和 to join nouns, such as two people or two foods. It does not normally join complete clauses. A joined subject can be followed by 都 when the statement applies to both members.
我喜欢苹果和香蕉。|I like apples and bananas.
爸爸和妈妈都在家。|Dad and Mom are both at home.
我有笔和纸。|I have pens and paper.
哥哥和姐姐都是老师。|My older brother and older sister are both teachers.
我买牛奶和面包。|I buy milk and bread.
猫和狗都很可爱。|Cats and dogs are both cute.
桌子上有书和报纸。|There are books and newspapers on the table.
我喝水和茶。|I drink water and tea.
小王和小李都是学生。|Xiao Wang and Xiao Li are both students.
我喜欢米饭和面条。|I like rice and noodles.

# Asking a matching question with 呢 | Person/topic + 呢？ | review
After a statement or question, use a short topic followed by 呢 to ask 'What about...?' The earlier context supplies the missing information.
我很好，你呢？|I am fine. How about you?
我喜欢茶，你呢？|I like tea. What about you?
我去学校，你呢？|I am going to school. What about you?
我有一只猫，你呢？|I have a cat. What about you?
我今天很忙，明天呢？|I am busy today. What about tomorrow?
我爸爸是老师，你爸爸呢？|My dad is a teacher. What about your dad?||我的爸爸是老师，你的爸爸呢？
我要水，你呢？|I want water. What about you?
我住北京，你呢？|I live in Beijing. What about you?||我住在北京，你呢？
我会游泳，你呢？|I can swim. What about you?
我姐姐在家，你姐姐呢？|My older sister is at home. What about your older sister?||我的姐姐在家，你的姐姐呢？

# Asking about ownership with 谁的 | 谁的 + noun / noun + 是谁的？ | review
Use 谁的 to ask whose something is. 谁的 can come before a noun, or stand alone after 是 when the object is already identified.
这是谁的书？|Whose book is this?
那是谁的杯子？|Whose cup is that?
这是谁的手机？|Whose phone is this?
那是谁的自行车？|Whose bicycle is that?
这支笔是谁的？|Whose is this pen?
这个苹果是谁的？|Whose is this apple?
这是谁的书包？|Whose schoolbag is this?
那是谁的电脑？|Whose computer is that?
这张票是谁的？|Whose is this ticket?
这些衣服是谁的？|Whose are these clothes?

# Asking small quantities with 几 | 几 + measure word + noun | review
Use 几 with a measure word to ask how many, usually when expecting a fairly small number. Keep the quantity phrase where the answer would go.
你有几个孩子？|How many children do you have?
她有几只猫？|How many cats does she have?
你要几个苹果？|How many apples do you want?
桌子上有几本书？|How many books are on the table?
你买几张票？|How many tickets are you buying?
他有几个哥哥？|How many older brothers does he have?
你喝几杯水？|How many glasses of water do you drink?
这里有几个学生？|How many students are here?
你家有几口人？|How many people are in your family?
你有几支笔？|How many pens do you have?

# Asking prices with 多少钱 | Item + 多少钱？ | review
Use 多少钱 to ask a price. Put the item first; 是 is usually unnecessary. A quantity before the item specifies how much you want priced.
这本书多少钱？|How much is this book?
这个杯子多少钱？|How much is this cup?
一杯茶多少钱？|How much is a cup of tea?
那件衣服多少钱？|How much is that item of clothing?
一张票多少钱？|How much is one ticket?
这个书包多少钱？|How much is this schoolbag?
一杯咖啡多少钱？|How much is a cup of coffee?
这支笔多少钱？|How much is this pen?
一斤苹果多少钱？|How much is one jin of apples?
这辆自行车多少钱？|How much is this bicycle?

# Pointing out objects with 这 and 那 | 这/那 + measure word + noun | review
Use 这 for 'this' and 那 for 'that'. Include a measure word before a singular noun, such as 本 for books, 杯 for cups of drinks, or 个 for many everyday objects.
这本书很小。|This book is small.
那只猫很可爱。|That cat is cute.
这个苹果很大。|This apple is big.
那杯茶很热。|That cup of tea is hot.
这件衣服很漂亮。|This item of clothing is pretty.
那个学生是我的朋友。|That student is my friend.
这张票是你的。|This ticket is yours.
那辆车很贵。|That car is expensive.
这支笔很好。|This pen is good.
那个杯子很小。|That cup is small.

# Pointing out groups with 这些 and 那些 | 这些/那些 + noun | review
Use 这些 for 'these' and 那些 for 'those'. They can directly precede a noun without an additional measure word. 都 can emphasize that a description applies to every member.
这些书是我的。|These books are mine.
那些苹果很大。|Those apples are big.
这些学生都是我的朋友。|These students are all my friends.
那些杯子很漂亮。|Those cups are pretty.
这些猫都很小。|These cats are all small.
那些衣服很贵。|Those clothes are expensive.
这些票是你的。|These tickets are yours.
那些老师都很好。|Those teachers are all nice.
这些香蕉很好吃。|These bananas are tasty.
那些椅子很旧。|Those chairs are old.

# Liking things and activities with 喜欢 | Subject + 喜欢 + noun/verb phrase | review
喜欢 can take a thing or an activity as its object. Use 不喜欢 for 'do not like'; do not add 是 before 喜欢.
我喜欢狗。|I like dogs.
她喜欢喝牛奶。|She likes drinking milk.
他喜欢看书。|He likes reading books.
我们喜欢这家饭店。|We like this restaurant.
妹妹喜欢画画。|My younger sister likes drawing.||我妹妹喜欢画画。
爸爸喜欢做饭。|Dad likes cooking.
我不喜欢咖啡。|I do not like coffee.
弟弟不喜欢吃鱼。|My younger brother does not like eating fish.||我弟弟不喜欢吃鱼。
你喜欢听音乐吗？|Do you like listening to music?
她喜欢学中文。|She likes learning Chinese.||她喜欢学习中文。

# Asking for things with 要 | Subject + 要 + noun | review
Use 要 followed by a noun to state what you want, especially when ordering or shopping. Adding 请 to a separate request can make it more polite; these examples practice the simple ordering pattern.
我要一杯水。|I would like a glass of water.
我要两张票。|I would like two tickets.
我们要三碗米饭。|We would like three bowls of rice.
她要一杯茶。|She would like a cup of tea.
我要这个苹果。|I would like this apple.
他要一碗面条。|He would like a bowl of noodles.
我要一杯热牛奶。|I would like a cup of hot milk.
我们要两个杯子。|We would like two cups.
你要什么？|What would you like?
我要那本书。|I would like that book.

# Stating age with 岁 | Person + number + 岁 | review
State an age by putting the number and 岁 after the person, without 是. 几岁 is common when asking a young child how old they are.
我今年十八岁。|I am eighteen years old this year.
弟弟五岁。|My younger brother is five years old.||我弟弟五岁。
妹妹三岁。|My younger sister is three years old.||我妹妹三岁。
我爸爸四十岁。|My dad is forty years old.||我的爸爸四十岁。
我妈妈三十八岁。|My mom is thirty-eight years old.||我的妈妈三十八岁。
姐姐二十岁。|My older sister is twenty years old.||我姐姐二十岁。
哥哥二十二岁。|My older brother is twenty-two years old.||我哥哥二十二岁。
她的儿子六岁。|Her son is six years old.
你女儿几岁？|How old is your daughter?||你的女儿几岁？
我爷爷七十岁。|My grandpa is seventy years old.||我的爷爷七十岁。

# Telling the clock time | 现在 + number + 点 (+ minutes) | review
Use 点 for the hour and 分 for minutes; 半 means half past. Chinese can state the current time without 是. Add 上午 or 下午 before the hour when needed.
现在八点。|It is eight o'clock now.||现在是八点。
现在九点半。|It is half past nine now.||现在是九点半。
现在十点十分。|It is ten ten now.||现在是十点十分。
现在七点二十分。|It is seven twenty now.||现在是七点二十分。
现在六点半。|It is half past six now.||现在是六点半。
现在十二点。|It is twelve o'clock now.||现在是十二点。
现在下午三点。|It is three in the afternoon now.||现在是下午三点。
现在上午十点。|It is ten in the morning now.||现在是上午十点。
现在四点五十分。|It is four fifty now.||现在是四点五十分。
现在几点？|What time is it now?||现在是几点？

# Asking when with 什么时候 | Subject + 什么时候 + verb phrase？ | review
Put 什么时候 before the verb to ask when an action happens. It occupies the same position as a time expression in the answer.
你什么时候回家？|When are you going home?
我们什么时候吃饭？|When are we eating?
她什么时候来？|When is she coming?
你什么时候上课？|When do you have class?
爸爸什么时候去北京？|When is Dad going to Beijing?
你什么时候睡觉？|When do you go to bed?
他什么时候起床？|When does he get up?
我们什么时候买票？|When are we buying tickets?
你什么时候看书？|When do you read books?
妈妈什么时候做饭？|When is Mom cooking?

# Going somewhere to do something | Subject + 去 + place + verb phrase | review
Put a destination after 去 and then add the activity you are going there to do. Chinese does not need a separate word corresponding to the English 'to' before that activity.
我去商店买水。|I am going to the store to buy water.
她去学校学中文。|She is going to school to study Chinese.||她去学校学习中文。
我们去饭店吃饭。|We are going to a restaurant to eat.
他去北京看朋友。|He is going to Beijing to see a friend.
我去医院看医生。|I am going to the hospital to see a doctor.
姐姐去商店买衣服。|My older sister is going to the store to buy clothes.||我姐姐去商店买衣服。
我们去公园跑步。|We are going to the park to run.
爸爸去学校接我。|Dad is going to school to pick me up.
我去图书馆看书。|I am going to the library to read.
他们去商店买水果。|They are going to the store to buy fruit.

# Doing things together with 一起 | Subject + 一起 + verb phrase | review
Place 一起 before the verb to say that people do something together. The subject can name the group or use a plural pronoun such as 我们.

他们一起学中文。|They study Chinese together.||他们一起学习中文。
我和妈妈一起做饭。|Mom and I cook together.||妈妈和我一起做饭。
我们一起看电影。|We watch a movie together.
她们一起去学校。|They go to school together.||他们一起去学校。
我和弟弟一起玩。|My younger brother and I play together.||弟弟和我一起玩。
我们一起喝茶。|We drink tea together.
爸爸和妈妈一起买菜。|Dad and Mom buy groceries together.||妈妈和爸爸一起买菜。
他们一起回家。|They go home together.

# Everyday frequency with 每 | 每 + time unit + 都 + verb phrase | review
Use 每 with a time unit to mean 'every'. 天 and 年 can follow 每 directly, while 星期 and 月 often take 个. 都 before the verb emphasizes the regular pattern.
我每天都喝牛奶。|I drink milk every day.
她每天都看书。|She reads books every day.
爸爸每天都做饭。|Dad cooks every day.
我们每个星期都去公园。|We go to the park every week.||我们每星期都去公园。||我们每周都去公园。
他每个月都买书。|He buys books every month.||他每月都买书。
我每年都去北京。|I go to Beijing every year.
妹妹每天都画画。|My younger sister draws every day.||我妹妹每天都画画。
我们每天都学中文。|We study Chinese every day.||我们每天都学习中文。
妈妈每天都喝茶。|Mom drinks tea every day.
我每个星期都看电影。|I watch a movie every week.||我每星期都看电影。||我每周都看电影。
