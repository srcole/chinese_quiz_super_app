# Learned ability with 会 | 会 + skill/action | review
Use 会 for a learned skill or knowing how to do something. Contrast 能 for ability under particular circumstances and 可以 for permission. 会 also has a separate predictive use.
我会游泳。|I know how to swim.
她会开车。|She knows how to drive.
你会做中国菜吗？|Do you know how to cook Chinese food?
他不会骑自行车。|He does not know how to ride a bicycle.
我妹妹会弹钢琴。|My younger sister can play the piano.
我们都会用这个软件。|We all know how to use this software.
你会写这个字吗？|Do you know how to write this character?
她会说三种语言。|She can speak three languages.
我还不会包饺子。|I do not know how to make dumplings yet.
他五岁就会下象棋了。|He already knew how to play Chinese chess at five.

# Circumstantial ability with 能 | 能 + verb | core
Use 能 for what is possible because of capacity or circumstances. It can sometimes overlap with permission, but these exercises focus on practical ability rather than knowing a learned skill.
我今天不能参加会议。|I cannot attend the meeting today.
这辆车能坐五个人。|This car can seat five people.
你能在六点以前回来吗？|Can you return before six?
我现在能走路了。|I can walk now.
他一天能读一百页。|He can read one hundred pages in a day.
这里能看到大海。|You can see the sea from here.
我听不清，你能大声一点儿吗？|I cannot hear clearly; can you speak a little louder?
没有地图，我们能找到吗？|Can we find it without a map?
她明天能来帮忙。|She can come and help tomorrow.
这个包能装很多东西。|This bag can hold many things.

# Permission with 可以 | 可以 + verb | review
Use 可以 to grant or ask permission. Negative permission is often 不可以 or 不能. 不用 instead means there is no need, so it does not express a prohibition.
我可以坐这里吗？|May I sit here?
你可以用我的电脑。|You may use my computer.
这里不可以拍照。|Photography is not allowed here.||这里不能拍照。
我们可以提前离开吗？|May we leave early?
你可以把书带回家。|You may take the book home.
我可以问一个问题吗？|May I ask a question?
孩子不可以一个人进去。|Children are not allowed to go in alone.
你可以在这里停车。|You may park here.
考试时不可以用手机。|You are not allowed to use a phone during the exam.
我可以试穿一下吗？|May I try it on?

# Advice with 应该 | 应该 + verb | core
Use 应该 for advice, expected duty, or a reasonable expectation. 不应该 means 'should not'. It is weaker than an explicit necessity such as 必须 in many contexts.
你应该早点睡觉。|You should go to bed earlier.
我们应该保护环境。|We should protect the environment.
他应该向你道歉。|He should apologize to you.
你不应该这样说。|You should not say that.
我应该先给她打电话。|I should call her first.
大家应该遵守规则。|Everyone should follow the rules.
你应该多喝水。|You should drink more water.
我们不应该浪费时间。|We should not waste time.
遇到问题应该及时沟通。|When problems arise, you should communicate promptly.
买东西以前应该比较价格。|You should compare prices before buying things.

# Necessity with 必须 | 必须 + verb | core
Use 必须 for a requirement or necessity. To say something is unnecessary, use 不必 or 不用, not an invented direct negative of 必须. Prohibitions use forms such as 不可以 or 不能.
你必须带护照。|You must bring your passport.
我们必须按时到。|We must arrive on time.
这件事必须今天解决。|This matter must be resolved today.
学生必须参加考试。|Students must take the exam.
进入工厂必须戴安全帽。|You must wear a safety helmet to enter the factory.
我必须先完成工作。|I must finish the work first.
所有人都必须登记。|Everyone must register.
这份文件必须由经理签字。|This document must be signed by the manager.
你必须认真考虑这个问题。|You must think carefully about this issue.
我们必须在月底以前搬走。|We must move out before the end of the month.

# No need with 不用 | 不用 + verb | core
Use 不用 to say that an action is not necessary. It does not forbid the action. 不必 is a common, somewhat more formal alternative with the same no-need meaning.
你不用等我。|You do not need to wait for me.||你不必等我。
今天不用上班。|There is no need to go to work today.
你不用带礼物。|You do not need to bring a gift.
我们不用提前买票。|We do not need to buy tickets in advance.
不用担心，我会处理。|There is no need to worry; I will handle it.
你不用回答这个问题。|You do not need to answer this question.
这里不用换鞋。|There is no need to change shoes here.
我不用再解释了。|I do not need to explain again.
孩子不用付钱。|Children do not need to pay.
你不用这么客气。|You do not need to be so formal.

# Wanting with 想 | 想 + verb | review
Use 想 before a verb to express a wish or intention, often softer than 要. 想 can also independently mean 'think' or 'miss', but these exercises focus on wanting to do something.
我想学做中国菜。|I want to learn to cook Chinese food.
她想换一份工作。|She wants to change jobs.
我们想去海边度假。|We want to vacation at the seaside.
你想喝点什么？|What would you like to drink?
我不想一个人去。|I do not want to go alone.
他想买一辆自行车。|He wants to buy a bicycle.
我想跟你谈谈。|I would like to talk with you.
孩子想再玩十分钟。|The child wants to play for another ten minutes.
我想了解中国文化。|I want to learn about Chinese culture.
她想在家休息一天。|She wants to rest at home for a day.

# Plans with 打算 | 打算 + verb phrase | core
Use 打算 before an action to state an intention or plan. It is a lexical verb, so it can also appear in questions about someone's plans or as a noun meaning 'plan'.
我打算明年去中国。|I plan to go to China next year.
你周末打算做什么？|What do you plan to do this weekend?
她打算毕业后找工作。|She plans to look for a job after graduation.
我们打算坐火车去。|We plan to go by train.
他不打算搬家。|He does not plan to move.
我打算每天练习半个小时。|I plan to practice for half an hour every day.
你打算住多久？|How long do you plan to stay?
他们打算开一家咖啡馆。|They plan to open a cafe.
我打算先存钱，再买车。|I plan to save money first, then buy a car.
我们打算邀请老师来参加。|We plan to invite the teacher to take part.

# Possibility with 可能 | 可能 + predicate | core
Place 可能 before the predicate for 'possibly' or 'may'. It expresses uncertainty and does not carry the learned-ability meaning of 会. It can also serve as an adjective meaning 'possible'.
明天可能会下雨。|It may rain tomorrow.
他可能忘了时间。|He may have forgotten the time.
她可能在开会。|She may be in a meeting.
这家店可能已经关门了。|This shop may already be closed.
我可能要晚一点儿到。|I may arrive a little late.
他们可能不来了。|They may no longer be coming.
钥匙可能在车里。|The keys may be in the car.
这个办法可能有用。|This approach may be useful.
你可能拿错包了。|You may have taken the wrong bag.
周末我们可能去爬山。|We may go hiking this weekend.

# Confident inference with 一定 | 一定 + predicate | core
Use 一定 to express a confident conclusion or a firm commitment, depending on context. 不一定 means 'not necessarily'; 一定不 means 'definitely not', so word order changes the scope.
他一定知道答案。|He must know the answer.
你一定很累了。|You must be tired by now.
我明天一定来。|I will definitely come tomorrow.
这一定是你的包。|This must be your bag.
她一定会喜欢这个礼物。|She will definitely like this gift.
我们一定能找到办法。|We can definitely find a way.
贵的东西不一定好。|Expensive things are not necessarily good.
他一定不会忘记。|He definitely will not forget.
你明天不一定有时间。|You will not necessarily have time tomorrow.
我一定按时完成。|I will definitely finish on time.

# Continuity with 一直 | 一直 + verb/state | core
Use 一直 to describe an action or state continuing through a relevant period. It can also mean 'straight ahead' with motion, but the examples here focus on continuous duration.
我一直在等你。|I have been waiting for you the whole time.
她一直住在北京。|She has been living in Beijing all along.
今天一直在下雨。|It has been raining all day today.
他一直没回我的消息。|He has not replied to my message all this time.
我们一直是好朋友。|We have always been good friends.
这个问题一直让我很担心。|This issue has been worrying me all along.
她一直工作到晚上十点。|She worked continuously until ten at night.
我一直想学画画。|I have always wanted to learn to draw.
门一直开着。|The door has been open the whole time.
他们一直在讨论旅行计划。|They have been discussing their travel plans the whole time.

# Only with 只 | 只 + verb/quantity phrase | core
Put 只 before the predicate or a quantity phrase to limit its scope: 'only'. It is distinct from 只有…才…, which marks a necessary condition, and from the classifier 只 pronounced zhī.
我只喝茶。|I only drink tea.
她只买了一本书。|She bought only one book.
我们只在周末见面。|We meet only on weekends.
他只会说一点儿中文。|He can speak only a little Chinese.
这张票只要二十块钱。|This ticket costs only twenty yuan.
我只想休息一下。|I only want to rest a little.
这里只有一个出口。|There is only one exit here.
她只告诉了我一个人。|She told only me.
我今天只睡了四个小时。|I slept for only four hours today.
这家店只卖水果。|This shop only sells fruit.

# Even with 连…都/也… | 连 + surprising item + 都/也 + predicate | core
Use 连 to highlight an extreme or unexpected example, followed by 都 or 也 before the predicate. The statement implies that less surprising cases are included as well.
他连自己的生日都忘了。|He even forgot his own birthday.
这道题连老师都不会做。|Even the teacher cannot solve this question.
我忙得连午饭都没吃。|I was so busy that I did not even eat lunch.
她连一句话都没说。|She did not say even a single sentence.
这个地方连地图上都找不到。|This place cannot even be found on a map.
孩子连鞋都没穿就跑出去了。|The child ran out without even putting on shoes.
他连水都不喝。|He will not even drink water.
我连他的名字都不知道。|I do not even know his name.
这里连冬天都很暖和。|It is warm here even in winter.
她连周末都要上班。|She has to work even on weekends.

# Unexpected contrast with 却 | Subject + 却 + contrasting predicate | core
Put 却 after the subject and before the predicate to mark a contrast with expectations. Unlike 但是, 却 is an adverb and does not normally stand before a new subject as a clause connector.
他很累，却睡不着。|He is tired, yet he cannot get to sleep.
这家店很小，东西却很多。|This shop is small, yet it has many things.
我给他打了电话，他却没接。|I called him, but he did not answer.
她年纪不大，经验却很丰富。|She is not very old, yet she has extensive experience.
大家都走了，他却留下了。|Everyone left, yet he stayed.
我想帮忙，却不知道该做什么。|I want to help, yet I do not know what to do.
虽然下雨，他却没带伞。|Although it was raining, he did not bring an umbrella.
这件衣服很贵，质量却不好。|This piece of clothing is expensive, yet its quality is poor.
他说不饿，却吃了两碗饭。|He said he was not hungry, yet he ate two bowls of rice.
我等了很久，车却一直没来。|I waited a long time, yet the bus never came.

# Mistaken assumptions with 以为 | 以为 + assumed proposition | core
Use 以为 to report a belief or assumption that turns out to be wrong or is being questioned. It often contrasts an earlier assumption with a later discovery; 觉得 is more neutral for a current opinion.
我以为今天是星期五。|I thought today was Friday, but I was mistaken.
她以为我已经走了。|She mistakenly thought I had already left.
我以为这家店很贵，其实很便宜。|I thought this shop was expensive, but it is actually cheap.
他以为考试取消了。|He mistakenly thought the exam had been canceled.
我以为你不会来。|I had thought you would not come.
我们以为坐地铁更慢，没想到更快。|We thought the subway would be slower, but unexpectedly it was faster.
她以为那是她的包。|She mistakenly thought that was her bag.
我以为你认识他。|I had assumed you knew him.
他以为只要十分钟，结果花了一个小时。|He thought it would take only ten minutes, but it ended up taking an hour.
我以为关门了，没想到还开着。|I thought it was closed, but unexpectedly it was still open.
